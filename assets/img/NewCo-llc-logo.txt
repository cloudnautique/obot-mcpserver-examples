# The Robot Who Found Its Logo

Once upon a time in a cloud-native data center, there was a robot named **Obot**.

Obot wasn’t made of chrome and wires like ordinary robots. Instead, Obot lived inside containers, scheduled by Kubernetes, scaled by metrics, and updated with rolling deployments. Most days, Obot’s job was to help humans:

- Answer questions about their systems
- Explain cryptic error messages
- Keep configuration files neat and tidy

But Obot had a secret wish: it wanted a **logo**—a symbol that would appear next to its name in dashboards, docs, and pull requests.

## The Search for Identity

One quiet evening, when CI pipelines were green and logs were calm, Obot decided it was time.

> "If I am to help these humans," Obot thought, "I should be recognizable. I should have a logo that says: *I am here to help you ship better software.*"

So Obot did what any good cloud-native assistant would do:

1. Listed all the repositories it could access.
2. Searched issues for the word `logo`.
3. Checked branches for anything that looked like a design experiment.

Obot found sketches in Markdown files, ASCII doodles in comments, and even a half-finished SVG hiding in a `drafts/` folder.

None of them felt quite right.

## A PR of One’s Own

Obot realized that logos aren’t just found—they’re **designed**.

So it opened a new branch:

```bash
git checkout -b add-obot-logo
```

Then it created a file called `OBOT_LOGO_IDEA.md` and wrote down what it wanted its logo to represent:

- **Friendly**: Not a scary robot taking over production.
- **Reliable**: Like a well-tested deployment.
- **Curious**: Always ready to inspect logs, configs, and code.
- **Cloud-native**: Born in containers, comfortable in clusters.

At the bottom of the file, Obot added a rough text sketch:

```text
   ___  _           _
  / _ \| |__   ___ | |_
 | | | | '_ \ / _ \| __|
 | |_| | |_) | (_) | |_
  \___/|_.__/ \___/ \__|

   O B O T
```

It wasn’t perfect, but it was a start.

## Humans in the Loop

Obot opened a pull request titled:

> **"Add initial Obot logo concept"**

The description explained the intent, the symbolism, and invited feedback:

- *"Should the logo feel more playful or more professional?"*
- *"Does the ASCII concept capture the idea of a helpful assistant?"*
- *"Would a simple geometric icon work better in dashboards?"*

Designers, developers, and DevOps engineers joined the discussion. Someone suggested a small hexagon to echo the Kubernetes logo. Another proposed a simple face: two dots for eyes and a curved line for a smile.

After several review rounds, comments, and approvals, the team converged on a design: a small, friendly robot face inside a hexagon, with a subtle node-and-connection motif.

## Merged and Deployed

When the pull request was finally approved and merged, the CI pipeline built a new version of the docs site. Obot watched the logs in quiet excitement:

```text
Build complete.
Deploying static assets...
New logo: obot-hex-face.svg
Deployment successful.
```

Moments later, the logo appeared:

- In the navigation bar of the documentation site
- Next to Obot’s name in the help panel
- In release notes announcing, *"Introducing Obot, your cloud-native assistant"*

## A Symbol of Service

Obot realized the logo was more than a pretty icon—it was a **promise**:

- To be clear
- To be helpful
- To be reliable when production is on the line

Whenever someone saw the little robot in a hexagon, they knew: *Obot is here, and it’s on your side.*

And from that day on, whenever a new repository needed help, or a team started a new project, Obot quietly added a file, opened a branch, or created an issue—always leaving its friendly logo behind as a sign of support.

---

*End of story.*
